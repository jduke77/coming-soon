Not inventory software

A system for never running out.

You don't need to count and barcode every screw like you're running a warehouse. Counting is for the things you sell. TwoBin is for the stuff that quietly keeps your shop running — consumables, hardware, the supplies nobody thinks about until they're gone.

That's what a two-bin system does. One bin in use, one in reserve. When the first runs empty, someone scans its card — and the reorder is on its way while the team keeps working out of the reserve.

Why TwoBin exists

Kanban was where it started.

I didn't come to lean from a business school. I came to it from a shop floor — mine — standing in a building I was proud of, watching it drift toward the kind of disorganized place I never wanted it to become.

So I went looking for shops that had figured it out, and I found a whole world of them — running more volume, more parts, more chaos than mine, and doing it calmly. They weren't special. They found a reason, and they started. And at the center of almost every one of them was the same humble thing. Not a consultant. Not an expensive system. A card, a bin, and a simple rule about when to reorder.

Kanban became the first domino for us. Once the shelves stopped running out, my team stopped firefighting and started improving. Organized bins led to organized benches. Standard reorders led to standard work. The morning meeting, the projects, the people stepping up — all of it was built on that one system holding.

“Kanban won't transform your shop by itself. Nothing will. But it's the rooted core you can build everything else on top of.”Jonathan Duke, founder
